Kosovo Labor Market Analysis 2012-2024

Author: Arnis Hasani

Tools: Python · pandas · matplotlib · ASK Data



What is this project?



This project analyzes Kosovo's labor market using official data from the 

Agency of Statistics of Kosovo (ASK) covering 2012 to 2024. I wanted to 

understand what is really happening with employment in Kosovo — not just 

what the official numbers say on the surface.



The data source is ask.rks-gov.net — Kosovo's official statistics agency.





What I found



The headline looks good — unemployment dropped from 30.9% in 2012 to 10.8% 

in 2024. But when I looked deeper, something didn't add up.



If unemployment dropped by 20 points, employment should have risen by roughly 

the same amount. It didn't. Employment only rose by 13 points.



That 7-point gap is the emigration signal. People didn't find jobs in Kosovo — 

they left Kosovo. When someone emigrates they are no longer counted in Kosovo's 

unemployment statistics, so the rate drops without the economy actually creating 

more jobs.



This means the official unemployment figures overstate how healthy Kosovo's 

labor market really is.



Other findings that stood out:



Youth unemployment peaked at 61% in 2014. By 2024 it dropped to 19.5% — 

which sounds like progress until you realize it is still 1.8x higher than 

the overall unemployment rate.



The NEET rate — young people not in employment, education or training — 

averaged 32% every single year from 2012 to 2024. 1 in 3 young Kosovars, 

consistently, going nowhere. This number barely moved in 12 years.



Kosovo's labor force participation is 43.2% in 2024. The EU average is 73%. 

Kosovo is 29.8 points behind — meaning more than half the working-age 

population is simply not participating in the economy at all.





What I would tell the government



The unemployment statistics are misleading. The real problem is not that 

people are unemployed — it is that people are leaving and that huge numbers 

of working-age people are not participating in the economy at all.



Bringing unemployment down by emigration is not a success. It is a warning.



The focus needs to be on why youth are leaving — low wages, few opportunities, 

lack of career growth. Until those drivers are addressed, the official numbers 

will keep improving while the actual workforce keeps shrinking.





Key numbers



Unemployment 2012: 30.9% → 2024: 10.8% (drop of 20.1 points)

Employment 2012: 25.5% → 2024: 38.6% (rise of only 13.1 points)

Emigration gap: 7 points

Youth unemployment 2024: 19.5% — 1.8x the overall rate

NEET rate average: 32% every year

Labor force participation: 43.2% vs EU average of 73%
